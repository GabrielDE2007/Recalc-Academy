About

Hi! I’m Gabriel, a sophomore at the University of Pennsylvania studying finance. I grew up in Newark, New Jersey, and I’m the first in my family to go to college.

Most of what’s on this page won’t be found on my resume: what I’ve been curious about recently, what I still don’t understand, and what I’ve learned from mentoring people who remind me of myself a few years ago.

Where it started When I was about twelve, my uncle started his own IT business. While visiting one day, I walked into my uncle’s basement and found it lined with shelves of IT parts, all organized in a way that only made sense once you understood why he’d priced and arranged them that way. I didn’t know it then, but that was probably the first time I got curious about how a business actually works.

How I think about businesses

When I walk into a restaurant or a hardware store, I always wonder why something costs what it costs. Is that price just covering the ingredients or materials, or is it also covering rent, insurance, and whatever else keeps the place running? What else is that business actually being paid for?

What I got wrong I used to assume a company’s cash on hand was basically revenue minus expenses. Of course, I have learned it’s not: financing changes everything.
Where a dollar goes

Loans, accounts payable, depreciation and amortization all mean a business can look very different on paper than it does in its bank account, and figuring out how those pieces fit together has become one of my favorite parts of any case or pitch.

I got to see this play out directly this summer, researching how Argentine sports clubs are financed compared to U.S. professional franchises while working in the audit department at RPA Consulting in Buenos Aires:

What I’m currently exploring

NUNubank

I first heard about Nubank in my Business Spanish class. It is a fintech that started by serving a LatAm market with limited access to traditional banking, and grew into one of the region’s biggest players. It’s now trying to enter the U.S., specifically targeting the Latino population here. I don’t know yet whether that move makes sense, and I’m still working through it. Beyond Nubank, I love learning about Latin companies and follow newsletters like LatinFinance to keep up with stories like this.

What I’ve worked on

RPA Consulting — Audit Assistant  /  Buenos Aires  /  Summer 2026

Audit work, automation, and a research report on the side

Some of it was exactly what you’d expect: reviewing financial statements, occasionally flagging a transaction that didn’t quite add up. But I also automated a manual process for generating and sending client letters, and researched how Argentine sports clubs are funded compared to their U.S. counterparts (see above). It was a good reminder that finance work isn’t only modeling, but a lot of it is process, research, and figuring out what actually matters in a pile of information.

BlackGen Capital — Analyst  /  Philadelphia

A stock pitch, and how much judgment a DCF actually takes

I worked with a small team to build a stock pitch: reading through financial statements, building a DCF valuation, and researching an industry from scratch. Our thesis came down to a licensing deal restructuring the company’s business model away from merchandise sales and toward licensing its brand. Presenting that recommendation to about 30 members was the first time I’d spoken in front of a group that size, and what stuck with me wasn’t the DCF itself, but how much judgment goes into deciding which assumptions are actually defensible.

I mentor college students through Wharton’s STEP and Penn First Plus FORGE programs, and high schoolers through Matriculate and Circle Match. Most of my mentoring with the highschoolers is on college applications and navigating a process that wasn’t built with a clear instruction manual for them.

One thing mentoring has taught me: structure matters just as much as showing up. I once went a month without being able to meet one of my mentees, and when we reconnected, they’d fallen noticeably behind. However, it was not because we hadn’t talked, but because I hadn’t given them anything concrete to work on in the meantime. I’ve tried to build real accountability into how I mentor since then, rather than assuming good intentions are enough.

Outside of mentoring, I’m also engaged with Penn’s Latino and first-gen community more broadly (I’m on the board of MEChA de Penn and our ALPFA chapter, and I recently joined FGLI Podcast Club, which brings first-gen professionals to campus to talk candidly about their experiences).
